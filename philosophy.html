Coherent writeup

Philosophy & nomenclature

Philosophy is rarely taught as a living craft. Our sites use a few atypical words on purpose. This page is the map: every unusual phrase on Intek Space should be clickable back here.

Spine (read once)

  1. All great works begin with a philosophy on life. Product is downstream of meaning.
  2. We consume death to breed life under stewardship—not ownership of the source.
  3. Standing goal: optimal land per life, per species—intelligence balanced with conversion efficiency—so the death-to-life breathe does not end in extinction.
  4. Method: philosophy → constraints → prototype → assemble · DMAIC · sector overlays · twin extremes.
  5. Public claims must match refused lists. Philosophy controls marketing; it is not decoration.

Glossary

Philosophy on life

The starting claim: every serious build begins with a view of what a living being is and what life is for— not with a market gap alone. Intek Space treats “philosophy first” as engineering discipline, not poetry on the side.

Consume death to breed life

Finite, ended things (food, fuel, matter, mortal givenness) are received and turned toward the generation and continuation of life: body, relation, garden, household, craft.

Related One Mission core (candidate): form and sustain living beings who have faith in the Creator and who consume death to breed life—without claiming ownership of the source.

Stewardship (not ownership)

We do not own the source of life. We care for systems we did not invent and cannot fully control. Design that pretends total ownership becomes extractive; design under stewardship stays honest about limits.

Death-to-life breathe / “the breathe”

A picture of conversion held in time: intake → convert → output → residual. “Breathe” is not a wellness brand. It is the loop by which life is sustained without lying about cost.

Breathe metrics measure those phases on real sites (energy, mass, labor, error correction)— not only lab-ideal data. If the breathe ends in extinction or hidden waste, the design failed.

Land per life (per species)

Standing goal metric: how much land (on-site and upstream feed/bran/etc.) is required to sustain life, by species, honestly. Intelligence may expand only if conversion efficiency keeps the breathe from eating the world empty.

Conversion system

Hardware + knowledge that turns inputs (sun, waste, feed, heat, work) into outputs that sustain life, while making land and energy cost visible. Gadgets without conversion honesty are not our product family.

Sector overlays

Site maps layered by real constraints: sun, water, wind, access, neighbor/risk, legal. Every install begins with overlays—not only a BOM.

Hardware moves only inside green/amber intersections. Red sectors (e.g. path to storm drain, illegal placement) are refusals, not “phase two.”

DMAIC

Define → Measure → Analyze → Improve → Control. Industrial improvement method applied to life systems.

  • D — problem boundary includes sectors and refusals before parts lists.
  • M — breathe metrics + land proxy + tagged sensors.
  • A — sector failures count (odor, splash) as much as electronics.
  • I — redesign toward twin extremes; publish updated refusals.
  • C — install packs require overlay + zone plan + datasheet; no scale-up on ideal drawings alone.

Twin extremes

We ship two poles, not the crowded middle:

Community · high-tech density — automation, shared infrastructure, dense practice.
Farmer · low-tech density — seasonal checklists, deep field practice, less automation.

Same philosophy, same refused lists. The middle market is elsewhere; we do not collapse to it by default.

Community density

High-automation, high-shared-infrastructure path. Control limits still tied to safety interlocks (e.g. drain/seal). Dense human practice is expected.

Farmer density

Low-automation path. Seasonal control plans, more manual intelligence, same honesty about land and residual. “Low-tech” does not mean careless.

Refused lists

Explicit “we will not claim / we will not place” items defined at Define stage. Examples: no storm-drain discharge; no human-food claims from uncontrolled waste; no medical diagnosis claims on body-signal education gear. Marketing may not override refusals.

Yard-to-Loop

A substantial field project (not a toy kit): conversion instrument for yard biomass / insect loops, designed under sector overlays and DMAIC. Student prototypes may grow toward Intek manufacture/assembly.
